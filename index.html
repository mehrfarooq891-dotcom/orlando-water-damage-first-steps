What Should You Do in the First 10 Minutes After Finding Water Damage?

The first minutes decide whether you are dealing with a drying job or a demolition job. In Florida's heat and humidity, mold spores begin colonizing wet drywall, carpet padding and wood framing within 24 to 48 hours — so the goal of the first 10 minutes is simple: stop the water, make the area safe, and create the record your insurance claim will need.

1. Shut off the main water supply

Stop the incoming flow first. Every minute a supply line runs is more water in the walls and floors. If the source is a single appliance with its own valve, close that; if in doubt, close the main. Know where your main shutoff is before an emergency — the middle of one is the worst time to learn.

2. Turn off power to wet rooms — if it is safe

Water and electricity are the dangerous combination in the house. Switch off breakers to wet rooms if you can reach the panel without stepping through standing water. If the panel itself is wet or the water is deep, stay out and let the restoration crew and, if needed, the utility handle it.

3. Photograph and video everything

Before any cleanup: clear photos and video of all standing water, the source if you can see it, and every affected item and surface. Insurers distinguish sharply between sudden damage, which standard Florida policies generally cover, and gradual damage, which they frequently exclude. Your photos, taken before mitigation, are the evidence of which one this is.

4. Then call — before you start cleaning up

Cleanup before documentation can complicate a claim, and household fans cannot dry a wall cavity in 70% ambient humidity. A restoration technician starts with thermal imaging and moisture mapping to find where the water actually went — behind baseboards, under tiled slabs, inside ceiling drywall — which is rarely only where you can see it.

What not to do

Do not tear out wet drywall or flooring yourself before it is documented and assessed: clean supply-line water damage can often be dried in place if extracted within 24 hours, while submerged laminate, saturated drywall and anything contaminated by sewage or floodwater has to come out. The assessment is what tells you which.
